1What HillDesk Is

A scheduling assistant for congressional offices that turns a flood of incoming email into organized, reviewable events.

Every day, a U.S. House office receives dozens of emails asking the Member of Congress to attend meetings, speak at events, or make appearances. Sorting through them by hand — figuring out which are actual scheduling requests, pulling out the date, time, location, and who's asking — is slow and easy to get wrong.

HillDesk does the first pass automatically. It reads incoming email, uses artificial intelligence to recognize scheduling requests and pull out the key details, and then hands everything to a staff member to review before anything becomes official. The AI never makes the final call — a person always does.

Think of it like: a very fast assistant who opens all the mail, highlights the important parts of each scheduling request, fills in a draft calendar entry, and leaves it on your desk with a note that says "please check this before I put it on the calendar." You stay in control; the assistant just removes the busywork.

Who uses it

Staff in a congressional office, working in three roles: Admins (run the office setup and manage people), Schedulers (own the calendar and approve changes), and Staff (handle events in their subject areas and suggest changes).

2How It Works

The journey of a single scheduling email, from inbox to approved calendar entry.

1
The email arrives. HillDesk connects to the office's Microsoft Outlook mailbox and pulls in new messages automatically — no copying and pasting.
2
The AI reads it. Artificial intelligence decides "Is this actually a scheduling request?" and gives a confidence score, so low-certainty guesses are flagged rather than trusted blindly.
3
The AI extracts the details. If it is a request, the AI pulls out the title, date, time, location, who's asking, and the topics involved — and remembers exactly which sentence each detail came from.
4
A draft event is created. The extracted details become a draft event marked "needs human review," waiting in the inbox.
5
A person reviews it. A staff member accepts or rejects each field the AI filled in. They can see the original email side-by-side. Every accept/reject is logged.
6
It goes through approval (if needed). The event can be routed to one or more people who must sign off — in parallel ("everyone approves") or in sequence ("first this person, then that one").
7
It lands on the calendar. Once approved, the event appears in the schedule and calendar views. A reply email can be sent back using a saved template.
The golden rule: the AI is assistive only. It drafts and suggests; it never decides. A human reviews and approves before anything is final. This "human-in-the-loop" design is deliberate and required for the office's compliance rules.

3The Building Blocks

HillDesk is built from a handful of separate parts, each with one job. Here's each one in plain terms.

The Web Application Django

The core program that runs everything — the pages staff see, the rules about who can do what, and the logic that ties it all together. It's the "engine" of HillDesk.

Built on Django, a mature Python web framework used by organizations worldwide.

The Four Departments (Apps)

Internally the engine is split into four focused areas, like departments in an office:

  • Authentication — handles logins, user accounts, roles, and office setup.
  • Email Processing — pulls in email and runs the AI.
  • Event Management — the calendar, events, approvals, and exports (the main screens).
  • API — the messenger that lets the screens talk to the engine behind the scenes.

The Filing Cabinet (Database)

Where all the information lives permanently — every event, email, user, and approval. It's organized and indexed so the app can find things instantly.

PostgreSQL in production (a robust, enterprise database); SQLite for local development.

The Back-Office Worker (Task Queue)

Some jobs take time — fetching a batch of email, running the AI, building a Word document. Rather than make staff wait, these are handed to a background worker that does them quietly and reports back when done.

Celery handles the background jobs; Redis is the fast "to-do list" they pull work from.

The AI Brain (Language Model)

The part that actually reads and understands email. HillDesk is designed so the AI can be swapped out depending on the setting — a secure government-cloud AI in production, or a fake stand-in for offline testing — without changing the rest of the app.

Amazon Bedrock (Claude models) in production; a "mock" provider for development. Outside AI services are blocked in production by compliance rules.

The Front Door (Authentication & Login)

Controls who gets in. In the real office, staff sign in with their existing Microsoft work account (single sign-on), so there's no separate password to remember. Optional two-step verification adds another layer.

Azure Active Directory single sign-on in production; standard username/password locally.

The Mail Connection (Microsoft Graph)

The secure link to the office's Outlook mailbox that lets HillDesk read incoming email and send replies.

Microsoft Graph API, the official way to connect to Microsoft 365.

4What It Can Do

The features staff actually use, day to day.

Automatic Email Intake & AI Extraction

Pulls in Outlook email, spots scheduling requests, and drafts event details automatically — with a confidence score so uncertain guesses get flagged.

Human Review of AI Suggestions

Every AI-filled field can be accepted or rejected individually, with the original email shown alongside. "SmartExtract" can re-run the AI on demand to suggest fields for an existing event. Every decision is recorded.

Event & Calendar Management

Create, edit, search, and organize events with full details — date, time, location, topics, attendees, point of contact, assigned staff, and tags. View them as a list or on a calendar (day/week/month).

Approval Routing

Send an event to one or more people for sign-off. Approvals can run in parallel (everyone must approve) or in sequence (one after another). Approvers can approve, or "send back" with a comment requesting changes.

Staff Change Suggestions

When a staff member who isn't assigned to an event wants to change it, their edit becomes a suggestion instead of an immediate change. A scheduler or admin reviews and approves or rejects it — protecting the official record.

Roles & Permissions

Three roles — Admin, Scheduler, Staff — control who can change statuses, manage people, configure approvals, and edit directly versus suggest.

Multiple Offices, Customized

Each office can define its own event statuses, types, formats, colors, and tags, plus Member and district details — so HillDesk matches how that specific office works.

Email Replies & Templates

Reply to requesters using saved, reusable templates (RSVPs, follow-ups, declines, thank-yous). The AI can even draft a reply for staff to review and send.

Exports

Produce polished Word documents (daily briefings, weekly overviews, event details) and spreadsheet (CSV) exports from office-customizable templates.

House Floor Schedule

Automatically pulls in the weekly "Bills This Week" floor schedule from the official House source, so staff can see legislative business alongside the calendar.

Archiving & Retention

Old events can be archived (hidden but kept) and managed under retention rules, keeping the working calendar clean without losing history.

Personal Settings

Each user controls their theme, default view, notifications, saved search filters, profile, and password.

5Keeping Data Safe

HillDesk handles sensitive congressional correspondence, so security is built into every layer.

Sensitive Information Is Scrambled

Personal details are encrypted in the database, so even someone with raw access to the files can't read them. Each record is also labeled by sensitivity (public, internal, confidential, restricted).

Everything Is Logged

Every change to an event, every AI accept/reject, every approval, and every login is recorded with who did it, when, and from where — a complete audit trail for accountability.

Strong Login Protection

Single sign-on with the office's Microsoft accounts, optional two-step verification, and automatic lockout after repeated failed login attempts.

Walled-Off Network

In production the app runs inside a private government cloud with no open door to the public internet. It talks only to approved services over a dedicated private connection.

AWS GovCloud, private network, Direct Connect — no public internet egress.

Approved AI Only

In production, only the government-approved AI service is allowed. The app actively refuses to start if someone tries to configure an outside AI provider.

Standard Web Protections

Encrypted connections, protections against common web attacks, and limits on how often requests can be made — the expected safeguards for a professional application.

HTTPS/HSTS, CSRF protection, Content Security Policy, rate limiting, secure cookies.

7Where the App Runs

HillDesk runs in different "modes" depending on whether it's the real office system or a developer's test setup. The mode decides which AI, login, and database it uses.

SettingProduction (real office)Development (testing)
AI brainAmazon Bedrock (approved gov-cloud AI)Mock stand-in, or test AI
LoginMicrosoft single sign-onSimple username/password
DatabasePostgreSQL (enterprise)SQLite (lightweight file)
NetworkPrivate government cloud, no public internetLocal computer
Background jobsReal queue (Celery + Redis)Run instantly, in-line
In short: the production system is locked down, encrypted, and government-compliant; the development setup is a safe sandbox that mimics the real thing without touching any real data or sensitive services.
